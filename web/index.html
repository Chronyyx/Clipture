Questions

What you need, what it costs, and where your clips go.

Is Clipture free?

Yes. Clipture is free and open source under the MIT license, with no account, subscription or watermark.

What do I need to run it?

Windows 10 or 11 and an NVIDIA graphics card. Clipture records with NVIDIA's NVENC hardware encoder, so AMD and Intel graphics are not supported yet.

How far back can it save?

Anywhere from 5 seconds to 10 minutes. Press Ctrl+Shift+S, or your own shortcut, and that much of what you just played is saved as an MP4 clip at 24, 30, 60 or 120 FPS.

Does it inject anything into my games?

No. Clipture captures through Windows' own screen capture and never hooks or loads code into the game process.

Will recording lower my frame rate?

The GPU's dedicated video encoder does the recording, and the replay waits on disk rather than in RAM. HDR games are tone mapped, so clips look right on any screen.

Where do my clips go?

To a folder you choose, with game, microphone and app audio on separate tracks. To share one, send a friend an invite link: the clip goes straight from your PC to theirs, encrypted, without a cloud upload.
